We can't do it alone.

We go from place to place to run errands, see friends, go to work, get food — all those things that keep us fed, productive, and happy. But when public transit infrastructure limits our mobility, the entire city feels it.

When public transportation is safe, robust, and energy-efficient, we create a healthier city. Seattle's trolleybus network is proof — an invisible architecture of wire above the street that most riders never look up to see.

"When our transit is energy-efficient,
we create a healthier city."

Route 44.
Clean, quiet, electric.

King County Metro's trolleybus fleet serves Seattle's densest corridors — a preview of what the entire system could be. Only 50% of the county's current bus fleet is electric. That gap is the opportunity.

Every vehicle, a choice.

At UW alone — 52,000 students, a substantial sample of Seattle — 68% use public transit weekly and 51% ride King County buses. Over 26,000 bus riders, one green ride at a time.
